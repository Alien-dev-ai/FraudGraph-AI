  - Fraud rate varies sharply by transaction type (see fraud_by_type) — this is a descriptive pattern in the raw labels, observed directly from the data.
  - Account activity counts (unique origin/destination accounts, accounts with repeat transactions) are descriptive summaries of the raw account-id fields.

--- Known PaySim simulator artifacts (not data defects) ---
  - isFlaggedFraud triggers on only a tiny subset of actual fraud cases (see flagged_vs_actual_fraud) — PaySim's isFlaggedFraud is documented as a deliberately weak, simplistic control (a fixed amount threshold), not a competitive fraud signal.
  - Merchant destination accounts (nameDest starting with 'M') report oldbalanceDest = newbalanceDest = 0 for effectively all such rows — PaySim does not simulate merchant balances by design (confirmed in Milestone 3).
  - A large share of origin/destination balance pairs do not satisfy simple arithmetic consistency (newBalance = oldBalance +/- amount), including many rows where both balances are reported as exactly zero — this is a well-documented PaySim simulator characteristic (Milestone 3 balance_consistency check), not a data-quality defect introduced by this pipeline.

--- Findings requiring later modeling to verify ---
  - Correlations between amount and balance fields (see amount_balance_correlations) are purely descriptive linear associations; whether these fields (or nonlinear combinations of them) are actually predictive of fraud requires a trained model to verify, not EDA alone.
  - Elevated fraud rates for TRANSFER/CASH_OUT versus other types are a strong candidate signal, but whether transaction type adds predictive value beyond amount/balance features (vs. being redundant with them) requires model evaluation.
  - Any time-based pattern in fraud_by_day is based on a small number of simulated days and should not be treated as a seasonality finding without further modeling/validation.
